Writing

TER · Inria DataShape & IMO Orsay · 2026

Generalizing the Cohen–Steiner curvature inequalities to arbitrary dimension

Supervised by Marc Glisse (Inria DataShape / Institut de Mathématiques d'Orsay), and graded 19/20. Starting from Cohen–Steiner's inequalities for the curvature of curves and surfaces, this work generalizes the curvature bounds to arbitrary dimension, using persistent homology, integral geometry (Crofton, Steiner, and Hadwiger's theorem), and Morse theory for manifolds with boundary. The main theorem bounds differences in Lipschitz–Killing curvatures by integrating the Euler characteristic over hyperplane sections.

Experience

TLD (ALVEST Group) · June–July 2025

Data analysis for error codes in electric aircraft tractors

Built a methodology to turn raw error logs into a diagnostic tool, applied to a fleet of electric towbarless tractors operated by a major European airline at Paris–Charles de Gaulle: DBSCAN temporal clustering to isolate error bursts, then two composite scores — a cause/effect axis estimated by fitting a Beta law to normalized in-burst positions, and a recurrent/impulsive axis from the spread of appearance days. A rank-based position metric was introduced to handle timestamp collisions, and validated against hierarchical clustering across seven time scales. Delivered as an interactive Dash interface for maintenance engineers.

NLP on customer feedback for a Lebanese bank's mobile app

Lebanon's financial crisis had badly eroded public confidence in banks, and the work was aimed at rebuilding that relationship on the consumer side. I applied NLP and machine learning to customer feedback on the bank's mobile application, structuring the resulting insights inside a graph database to surface the issues driving users away. It was my introduction to graph databases, and to modelling a problem as a network of relations rather than a table of rows — which has shaped how I approach data since.
