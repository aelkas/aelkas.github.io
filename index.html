Inria

Research internship (TER) · Inria DataShape & IMO Orsay · Apr–Jun 2026

Generalizing the Cohen–Steiner curvature inequalities to arbitrary dimension

Supervised by Marc Glisse (Inria DataShape / Institut de Mathématiques d'Orsay), and graded 19/20. Starting from Cohen–Steiner's inequalities for the curvature of curves and surfaces, this work generalizes the curvature bounds to arbitrary dimension, using persistent homology, integral geometry (Crofton, Steiner, and Hadwiger's theorem), and Morse theory for manifolds with boundary. It also discusses limits to such results. The main theorem bounds differences in Lipschitz–Killing curvatures by integrating the Euler characteristic over hyperplane sections.

TLD

TLD (ALVEST Group) · June–July 2025

Data analysis for error codes in electric aircraft tractors

Built a methodology to turn raw error logs into a diagnostic tool, applied to a fleet of electric towbarless tractors operated by a major European airline at Paris–Charles de Gaulle: DBSCAN temporal clustering to isolate error bursts, then two composite scores — a cause/effect axis estimated by fitting a Beta law to normalized in-burst positions, and a recurrent/impulsive axis from the spread of appearance days. A rank-based position metric was introduced to handle timestamp collisions, and validated against hierarchical clustering across seven time scales. Delivered as an interactive Dash interface for maintenance engineers.

Projects

MVA coursework · PyTorch

DiffusionNet for RNA surface segmentation

An implementation of DiffusionNet (Sharp et al., 2022) that labels every vertex of an RNA molecule's surface mesh with one of 160 classes. Features spread over the surface by a heat equation whose diffusion time is learned, solved in the basis of the first 128 Laplacian eigenvectors. It reaches 82.2% validation accuracy from raw coordinates and 84.6% with heat kernel signature inputs. Without the learned diffusion it falls to 30.1%.

Independent · from scratch

Shaaer, an Arabic poetry transformer

A GPT trained from scratch on classical Arabic poetry, following the nanoGPT approach, with a grapheme-cluster-aware tokenizer for Arabic. Moving from a 2.65M-character corpus to a 162M-character one brought validation loss from 2.68 to 2.25 bits per character. The write-up also covers the tokenizer and model-width sweeps, including what they can't conclude.
